# Backlink Acquisition Plan — visaindiaexpert.com

**Target: 5 quality backlinks (referring domains) per month** for 90 days
(Oct–Dec 2026 → 15 total). This is a planning target, not a forecast: links
arrive with lag, depend on editor and reporter responses, and no specific
rankings or timelines are guaranteed. Links are one of many ranking factors.

## (a) Goal + rules

**Goal.** Five genuinely relevant referring domains per month, pointing to the
most relevant page (post → its /visa/* hub), with durable, editorially placed
links — not placements that rot.

**Rules**

1. **No PBNs, link farms, bought links or paid placements.** Only editorial
   links, client reviews, and our own brand profiles.
2. **Relevance > volume.** One link from an immigration/startup/expat site
   beats ten from directories. If a "link opportunity" has no topical fit,
   skip it.
3. **Anchor mix** — keep it natural, never exact-match on money keywords:
   - branded: "Visa India Expert" (~40%)
   - generic: "visa assistance", "this guide", "the service" (~40%)
   - keyword: "india visa assistance" (~20%, only where it reads naturally)
4. **No link exchanges** with competitor or client sites.
5. **Track every link** in the log (section f): date, source URL, anchor,
   target page, type (review / guest post / press / community / profile).
6. **Honest expectations.** Most links will not move rankings directly within
   90 days; the compounding value (referral traffic, authority, AI-citation
   sources) is the point. Never promise a client or stakeholder a specific
   ranking from a specific link.

## (c) HARO / Qwoted

**What it is.** Reporters (newsletters, business, immigration and travel
outlets) post queries asking for expert quotes. HARO (Help a Reporter Out)
pioneered the model; the active platforms today are **Qwoted**, **Featured**
and **Help a B2B Reporter**. A quote can become a bylined or unattributed
citation with a link to your site — one of the cheapest high-DR links
available, and a strong AI-citation source (see docs/ai-search.md).

**Free tiers.** All three have free tiers: Qwoted free allows a small number
of responses per day (paid tier raises the cap); Featured and Help a B2B
Reporter are free via email digests. Start on free everywhere; upgrade only
if we are consistently converting queries into citations and the free cap
becomes the bottleneck.

**How to register (30 min, once).**

1. Sign up on all three with the site's email (one inbox for all).
2. Set category to Immigration / Travel / Business.
3. Bio (used by all three): *"India visa specialist — 100+ applications
   filed across tourist, business, medical, E-1, spouse and employment
   categories. Founder of Visa India Expert (visaindiaexpert.com), a
   specialist India visa assistance service with transparent
   kickoff+success pricing."*
4. **Daily, 10 min:** scan that morning's queries; respond to matching ones
   (immigration, India business setup, expat life, founder travel) **within
   2 hours** — reporters pick from the first responses.

**Response template (~150 words).** Adapt the bracketed parts; keep it under
150 words total.

```
Subject: Re: [query] — India visa expert

Hi [name],

Quick answer: [1–2 sentences that answer the question directly, with one
specific fact or number].

A bit more context: [2–3 sentences — the nuance, the common mistake, or the
2026-specific rule that makes this timely].

Happy to expand with more detail or jump on a quick call. I'm the founder of
Visa India Expert (visaindiaexpert.com), a specialist India visa assistance
service; I've filed 100+ applications across tourist, business, medical,
E-1 and spouse categories, and I write weekly about the process at
visaindiaexpert.com/blog.

Best,
[Name]
```

### d.2 — HARO / Qwoted journalist answer

Use the 150-word response template from section (c). Ready-to-send tips:
answer the exact question in the first line (reporters skim), include one
specific number or 2026 rule, and always end with the offer to expand. Send
within 2 hours of the query; log every response in the backlink log even if
it doesn't convert.

## (e) Monthly cadence (week-by-week, 5 links/month)

Assumes ~10 min/day on HARO/Qwoted, 2 outreach emails/week, 1 community
value post/week. Links lag outreach by 1–4 weeks, so the month's count is
often the tail of the previous month's work — hit the cadence, not the
number.

**Week 1**

- Mon: Clutch profile built/completed (target #1); send 2 guest-post pitches
  (#4, #5) with template d.3.
- Tue: schedule/execute the Product Hunt launch (#3); 1 value answer on
  r/AskIndia (#9).
- Wed: ask 1 happy client for a Clutch review (d.1); start the daily HARO
  routine (1–2 responses).
- Thu: 1 value post on InterNations or VisaJourney (#8/#10).
- Fri: check GSC → Links → Top linking pages; log anything new.
- *Expected live links: 0–1* (profile-type links).

**Week 2**

- Follow up on the 2 guest-post pitches (one short email each).
- 2 community value posts (rotate r/AskIndia / VisaJourney / InterNations);
  daily HARO.
- Ask a 2nd client for a Clutch review (d.1).
- *Expected live links: 1–2* (Clutch profile + first review; a community
  profile link).

**Week 3**

- Final follow-up on pitches; if an editor replied, deliver the draft within
  48h.
- 2 community value posts; daily HARO.
- *Expected live links: 1–2* (first guest post accepted → publish this week
  or next; a HARO citation if a query converted).

**Week 4**

- Measure: GSC Links report + manual checks; log the month's links in the
  table below.
- Plan next month's targets (Nov: law-firm pitch #6, The Migrant #7, Indie
  Hackers #14, Show HN #15).
- *Expected live links: 1–2* (guest post published; 2nd Clutch review).

**Realistic month math:** 5 links ≈ 2 guest posts + 2 Clutch reviews +
1 HARO citation — or month 1: Product Hunt + Clutch profile + 2 guest posts +
1 community/profile link. If a month lands at 3, the gap is almost always
unanswered HARO queries or un-followed-up pitches, not the target list.
